# Interview Preparation

## 60-second project explanation
"I developed a self-initiated Business Analyst portfolio case study for a fictional retail bank that wanted to improve its loan application and EMI payment portal. I defined the business problem, stakeholders, scope and objectives, then prepared a BRD and translated the business needs into functional requirements. I created user stories with acceptance criteria, mapped AS-IS and TO-BE workflows and conceptual DFDs, organized a sample Agile backlog and sprint plan, and prepared UAT cases and an RTM. The project helped me understand how a BA connects business needs with development and testing."

## Questions you should be ready to answer
1. Why did you choose this project?
2. Who are the stakeholders and what information would you collect from each?
3. What is the difference between BRD and FRD?
4. What makes a user story testable?
5. Explain one acceptance criterion using Given–When–Then.
6. What is the difference between AS-IS and TO-BE?
7. What does a context-level DFD show?
8. How would you prioritize the backlog?
9. What is the purpose of an RTM?
10. How would you handle a payment that remains pending?
11. What is the difference between preliminary eligibility and final loan approval?
12. What would you validate with Compliance and Risk?

## Example answer: pending payment
"I would not show a payment as successful just because the customer clicked Pay. The portal should wait for a verified provider response. If there is a timeout, it should remain Pending while the system follows the agreed reconciliation process. This avoids misleading the customer and reduces duplicate-payment risk."

## Be precise about tools
If you create a Jira project, explain the epics, stories, priorities, statuses and sprint plan you configured. If you only maintain the sample backlog in this repository, say that you prepared a Jira-style backlog as a simulation and are familiar with how it maps into Jira. Apply the same honesty to Confluence and draw.io.
